# REPAIR_ANALYSIS.md

**What can this dataset change, what would it be worth, and which intervention is the most
economically efficient — computed from evidence only.**

Companion documents: `MULTI_DATASET_ARCHITECTURE.md` (case files, isolation),
`FINAL_REPORT_FORMAT.md` (where repairs appear in the report), `DATASET_GUIDE.md`
(what to put in your CSV so the engine has something to work with).

---

## 1. Two questions, answered separately

```text
        ┌───────────────────────────────┐
        │ 1. WHAT CAN BE CHANGED?       │   discovered from THIS dataset
        │    (candidate levers)         │   (columns, or the documented route)
        └───────────────┬───────────────┘
                        ↓
        ┌───────────────────────────────┐
        │ 2. WHAT IS IT WORTH?          │   measured quantities × stated rates
        │    expected loss reduction    │
        └───────────────┬───────────────┘
                        ↓
        ┌───────────────────────────────┐
        │ 3. DOES IT PAY BACK?          │   net impact = reduction − intervention cost
        │    (net impact, confidence)   │   intervention cost comes ONLY from the rate card
        └───────────────┬───────────────┘
                        ↓
        CHEAPEST SUPPORTED EFFECTIVE REPAIR
        (highest net benefit per unit of intervention cost,
         among repairs whose effect AND cost are both supported)
```

There is no universal repair list. A lever is offered only when the dataset (or the
documented-route simulation, for the supplied archives) provides a quantity to act on.

## 2. Candidate discovery

### 2a. From the dataset's own columns (`_column_repairs`)

| Lever | Column the profiler must have found | Measured quantity |
|---|---|---|
| Reduce downtime | a duration column (`downtime_hours`, `downtime_min`, …) | mean hours lost per row, scaled to the row count |
| Reduce scrap | a scrap count or cost column | units/cost per row |
| Reduce rework | a rework count or cost column | units/cost per row |
| Reduce queue / WIP | a queue or WIP column | mean parts waiting |
| Reduce cycle time | a cycle-time column | mean seconds per unit |

Units are taken from the column name, never assumed in the wrong direction:
`_hours_from_column` reads `_h`/`hour` as hours, `_min`/`minute` as minutes (÷60) and
`_s`/`sec` as seconds (÷3600); a duration whose unit the name does not state is reported with
the reason instead of a number. `_unit_hint` does the same for currency and counts.

### 2b. From the documented-route simulation (`_sim_repairs`, supplied archives only)

For `model1`/`model2`/`model3`, a candidate is also generated by re-running the
discrete-event simulation with a changed parameter (capacity, demand factor, cycle time) and
comparing the result with the baseline: throughput, WIP, queue deltas and the simulation's
own validation residual. These candidates carry `scenario` and the simulation's evidence, so
the effect is a *simulated* one, labelled as such.

## 3. Where the money comes from

Every rate is either **measured in the dataset** or **supplied in the rate card** — never
guessed. `cost_context()` resolves them in this order and records the provenance of each one:

| Concept | Dataset column (case A) | Rate-card field (case B) |
|---|---|---|
| Scrap | `scrap_cost`, `scrap_cost_per_unit`, `cost_per_unit_scrapped` | `cost_per_unit_scrapped` |
| Rework | `rework_cost`, `rework_cost_per_unit` | `rework_cost_per_unit` |
| Downtime | `downtime_cost`, `downtime_cost_per_hour` | `downtime_cost_per_hour` |
| Holding | — | `holding_cost_per_unit_hour` |
| Unit value | `margin`, `margin_per_unit`, `unit_price`, `price_per_unit`, `revenue`, `unit_value` | `margin_per_unit` |

A rate card **overrides** a dataset column for that dataset only. Currency is taken from the
rate card, else from a `currency`-like column (INR/USD/EUR/GBP/₹/$/€/£), else reported as
"currency not stated" — no currency is invented.

**Case A — the dataset contains costs.** Rates are read from the columns and each line says
so: `"dataset column 'scrap_cost' (mean)"`. Dataset A of the demo (`scrap_cost`,
`downtime_cost`, `unit_value`) is this case.

**Case B — the dataset has no costs.** Candidates are still produced and their *measured*
effects are still reported, but every monetary field is `null` and the page says why.
Dataset B of the demo (no scrap/rework/downtime cost) is this case: the engine answers

> Repair cost comparison unavailable until a rate card is provided.

and the Repairs page offers the rate-card form.

**Intervention cost** is *never* in a dataset — a plant export does not know what a project
costs. It comes only from the rate card (`intervention_cost_fixed`,
`intervention_cost_per_capacity_unit`). Without one, `intervention_cost.amount` stays `null`
and that candidate cannot be ranked.

## 4. Selection rule

```python
priced = [c for c in candidates
          if c["expected_loss_reduction"]["amount"] is not None
          and c["intervention_cost"]["amount"] is not None
          and c["net_impact"]["amount"] > 0]

best = max(priced, key=lambda c: (net_impact / intervention_cost, confidence_rank))
```

So the chosen repair is the **cheapest supported effective repair**: the highest net benefit
per unit of intervention cost among repairs whose effect and cost are *both* supported by this
dataset, with confidence as the tie-breaker. It is **not** simply the one with the lowest
price — a cheaper intervention that does not solve the problem is never selected.

Three honest outcomes:

| Status | When | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| `available` | at least one candidate has a valued effect, a cost and a positive net impact | "Cheapest supported effective repair: <repair> (net <amount> <currency> at a benefit-cost ratio of <x>x)." |
| `cost_comparison_unavailable` | candidates exist but no intervention cost is known | "Repair cost comparison unavailable until a rate card is provided." |
| `no_positive_net_impact` | every candidate is valued but none pays back | "…does not pay back its intervention cost with the data and rates supplied. Nothing is recommended on this evidence." |
| `unavailable` | no numeric process or cost column at all | "No supported repair can be proposed…" |

## 5. What every candidate reports

```text
Repair                          e.g. "Cut downtime by 20%"
Target                          station / column it acts on
Measured                        the quantity it reduces, and how it was measured
Expected quality effect         e.g. scrap units removed per row
Expected throughput effect      where the simulation supports it
Expected loss reduction         amount + currency + the rate each line used
Intervention cost               amount + basis, or null with the reason
Net impact                      reduction − intervention cost
Confidence                      high / medium / low
Evidence                        the measured facts behind it
Assumptions                     e.g. the assumed reduction percentage
```

Assumed reduction percentages are fixed, stated defaults (`DEFAULT_REDUCTIONS`: downtime 20%,
scrap 10%, rework 10%, queue 20%, cycle time 10%) and are returned with every candidate as
`reductions_assumed`, plus the limitation "Reduction percentages are stated assumptions, not
measurements — change them by supplying your own plan."

## 6. Worked example (live, 2026-09-20)

**Dataset A** (`factory_batch_a.csv`, 720 rows: 4 stations × 180 shifts; columns
`cycle_time_s, station_util, queue_len, downtime_hours, scrap_count, rework_count, scrap_cost,
downtime_cost, unit_value`):

* rates from dataset columns — `scrap_cost` 118.00/unit, `downtime_cost` 340.00/hour,
  `unit_value` 96.00/unit;
* rate card stored for this dataset supplies the intervention cost;
* candidates: cut downtime, cut scrap, cut rework, reduce queue/WIP, reduce cycle time;
* selected: **Cut downtime by 20%** — the highest net benefit per unit of intervention cost
  among the supported, priced, positive-net repairs (benefit-cost ratio ≈ 8.4×).

**Dataset B** (`factory_batch_b.csv`, 720 rows: 3 presses × 240 shifts; columns
`Cycle_Time_min, util_pct, queue_parts, downtime_min, defects, rework_units, unit_price`):

* no scrap/rework/downtime cost column → no rates can be measured;
* candidates are still listed (cut downtime, cut rework, reduce queue/WIP, reduce cycle time)
  with their measured quantities, every monetary field `null`;
* `status = cost_comparison_unavailable`; the page shows the rate-card form instead of a
  ranking. Nothing is guessed.

| Overview card | the selected repair, or "No supported repair yet" with the reason |

## 8. Limitations (also returned by the API)

* Intervention costs are never present in a dataset; they come from the rate card only.
* Reduction percentages are stated assumptions, not measurements.
* A repair whose effect depends on a re-simulation is only offered for the datasets the
  documented-route engine covers; for an uploaded CSV the effect comes from measured columns.
* Economic impact stays unavailable until rates exist — the app says so in one sentence
  rather than publishing a zero.
